      Socioeconomic Analytics Pipeline Workflow

      Visual map illustrating stages required to clean country parameters, train classification algorithms, and generate development predictions.

      01 📁

      Dataset Collection

      COMPLETED

      Compiling national indicators from raw socioeconomic datasets.

      Our source database collects yearly country stats from United Nations data repositories, providing a 1,922-row foundation mapping Life Expectancy, schooling factors, and national income spanning over two decades.

      ↓
      02 🧼

      Data Cleaning

      COMPLETED

      Filtering target indexes and removing duplicate data logs.

      We check for duplicate records across indices and purge incomplete target labels. Rows with missing HDI targets are removed to ensure models only learn from complete, verified datasets.

      ↓
      03 🧩

      Handling Missing Values

      COMPLETED

      Imputing gaps using country-specific chronological median values.

      Empty spaces are filled using the median values computed for that country chronologically. If a country possesses no history for an indicator, a global feature median is applied.

      ↓
      04 🎯

      Feature Selection

      COMPLETED

      Extracting critical features and transforming wealth scales.

      We select key indices ('Life_Expectancy', 'Expected_Schooling', 'Mean_Schooling') and perform a natural log transform on GNI per Capita to linearize correlation bounds and reduce outlier leverage.

      ↓
      05 ⚖

      Feature Scaling

      COMPLETED

      Standardizing indices to zero mean and unit variance.

      Features differ widely in units (years vs. thousands of dollars). We implement a StandardScaler so that distance-based classifiers (SVM, KNN) are not biased by larger scalar ranges.

      ↓
      06 ✂

      Train / Test Split

      COMPLETED

      Partitioning 75% for model training and 25% for evaluation.

      We perform a stratified split to ensure each developmental category (Low, Medium, High, Very High) is proportionally represented in both training and test partitions.

      ↓
      07 🤖

      Model Training

      COMPLETED

      Training 5 classifiers and 1 regressor model.

      Algorithms trained: Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, Support Vector Machine, and K-Nearest Neighbors. We also train a RandomForestRegressor to predict continuous scores.

      ↓
      08 📊

      Model Evaluation

      COMPLETED

      Benchmarking using F1, Accuracy, Precision, and Recall.

      Models are evaluated against the unseen 25% test set. We construct confusion matrices and classification reports for each classifier to identify performance trends.

      ↓
      09 🏆

      Best Model Selection

      COMPLETED

      Selecting the model with the highest weighted F1 score.

      Based on performance benchmarks, the Support Vector Machine was selected as our primary classifier, achieving the highest F1 weighted score of 98.95%.

      ↓
      10 💾

      Model Saving

      COMPLETED

      Serializing the best model and scaler using Joblib.

      The chosen SVM classifier, feature scaler, and companion Random Forest Regressor are serialized as binary files inside the models/ directory for live web server usage.

      ↓
      11 🔮

      Prediction

      ACTIVE

      Processing user parameters to project development levels.

      Inputs from the Scenario Simulator or Sample list are passed to our models via Flask, generating immediate HDI scores and confidence intervals.

      The UNDP Human Development Index (HDI) Formula

      An explanation of how the United Nations computes national development indices.

      The HDI is a summary measure of average achievement in key dimensions of human development: a long and healthy life, being knowledgeable, and having a decent standard of living. It is calculated as the **geometric mean** of normalized indices for each of the three dimensions:

      HDI = (Health_Index × Education_Index × Income_Index) ^ (1/3)

      1. Long & Healthy Life (Health Index)

      Assessed by Life Expectancy at Birth. It is normalized relative to a minimum of 20 years and a maximum of 85 years.

      Health Index = (Life Expectancy - 20) / (85 - 20)

      2. Knowledge Base (Education Index)

      Computed as the arithmetic mean of two sub-indices: Expected Years of Schooling (normalized between 0 and 18 years) and Mean Years of Schooling (normalized between 0 and 15 years).

      Education Index = (Mean_School_Index + Expected_School_Index) / 2

      3. Standard of Living (Income Index)

      Measured by GNI per Capita (PPP $). The logarithmic scale is normalized relative to a minimum of $100 and a maximum of $75,000.

      Income Index = (ln(GNI) - ln(100)) / (ln(75,000) - ln(100))

      Help & Platform Guide

      Frequently Asked Questions

      What features are required for predictions?

      The system utilizes 4 standard UNDP coefficients: Life Expectancy, Expected Schooling, Mean Schooling, and GNI per Capita (PPP $).

      How does the Live Scenario Simulator work?

      Adjusting any slider sends a non-blocking JSON request to our Flask backend. The server runs the scaled values through the trained ML models and returns predicted results instantly.

      How is confidence computed?

      Confidence corresponds to the classification probability returned by our Support Vector Machine classifier's predict_proba output.
